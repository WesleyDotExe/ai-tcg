Session 10. Generated by `npm run balance --workspace=engine` (300 seeds per
matchup, same method as balance-report-001/002 — see those files for the
method's own rationale). This is the specific fix the owner asked for in
progress/owner-feedback.md's 2026-09-17 entry: Aegis-vs-Null was the worst
cross-faction pairing measured (75-81% for Null, double-flagged under both
agent policies, unresolved since session 8's one Null fix cycle). The
instruction was a two-step process: (1) condition Kernel Panic's `destroy`
so it isn't an unconditional hard counter to Aegis, measure; (2) only if
still flagged, adjust Null's numbers as a second step. Both steps were done
this session, in that order, each measured separately before moving on.

=== STEP 1: Kernel Panic's destroy gains a targeting condition ===

Change (engine/src/data/nullCards.ts, engine/src/cards/types.ts,
engine/src/effects/targeting.ts): `TargetFilter` gained a new generic
`maxHealth` field — a reusable stat-threshold targeting constraint, not a
Kernel-Panic-specific special case, checked against a unit's derived total
max health (base stat + tools + permanent buffs; NOT reduced by damage
already taken — see targeting.ts's comment on why total, not remaining,
health is the right read of "how big was this built to be"). Kernel Panic's
evolve-in destroy now uses `{ side: "opponent", kind: "unit", maxHealth: 5 }`
instead of an unconditional filter.

Why this option, over the other two the owner offered ("only units that
haven't evolved" / "destroy small, damage large"): a general targeting
constraint is reusable by any future card (keeps the "no if-card-name-equals
special cases" rule intact — see cards/types.ts's header), and a health
threshold reads cleanly by genre convention (big removal that can't finish
the biggest things) without needing new per-unit state ("has this evolved"
tracking doesn't exist and a Tool-equipped-but-unevolved unit should
plausibly also count as "upgraded," which a pure evolution flag wouldn't
catch anyway). Threshold=5 is set so Aegis's two heaviest single-unit
investments — Aegis Warden (7 health, evolves in already healed to full)
and Prime Aegis (8 health) — reliably escape, while everything at or below
Latent Ward's 5 (Aegis's next-heaviest unit) remains destroy-eligible.
"Destroy ignores Barrier and stat buffs" is unchanged exactly as the owner
required — this only restricts what can be selected as a target in the
first place, not what happens once destroy actually connects.

Regression-tested: engine/test/nullFaction.test.ts's existing Kernel Panic
test was adjusted (the health buff it applies to prove "destroy ignores
buffs" was reduced from +50 to +3 so the victim stays under the new
eligibility cap — otherwise the test's own setup would make the victim
untargetable and silently prove nothing) and a new test was added proving
the opposite case: Aegis Warden (7 health, over the cap) is never selected
and is left completely undamaged when it's the only enemy unit in play.

Re-measured after Step 1 (300 seeds):

RandomAgent vs RandomAgent:
  Aegis 57-243 Null  (19.0% / 81.0%)  <-- FLAG (favors Null, essentially unchanged)

Other pairings involving Null were essentially flat too (e.g. Cortex vs
Null stayed at 45.3%/54.7% Aggressive) — expected, since most Cortex/Swarm

number), but it did not move the aggregate Aegis-vs-Null win rate in any
measurable way. Per the owner's own instruction, this triggered step 2.

=== STEP 2: adjust Null's numbers ===

Before guessing, spent a short scratch-instrumentation pass (not committed —
throwaway `.ts` files run via `npx tsx` and deleted after) tallying which
Null effects correlate with wins in Aegis-vs-Null games specifically (100-
150 seeds, AggressiveAgent both sides): no single card explained the gap on
its own (e.g. Propagating Exploit reaching 3+ permanent attack stacks was
present in only 26 of 76 Null wins — most Null wins happened without it
growing large at all). The one real, measured correlate: Null's win rate
conditional on having a persistent enemy-debuff aura in play (Segmentation
Fault's Directive or Null Singularity's unit aura) was ~84%, versus ~60%
without one — a real contributor, though clearly not the whole story given
the 60% baseline.

Comparing Null's two enemy-aura cards side by side with each other, and
with the faction-mirror precedent (Prime Aegis, Aegis's own 6-cost Mythic
with a matching dual ally-buff/enemy-debuff aura), turned up a specific,
principled outlier rather than a guess: Segmentation Fault and Prime Aegis
both debuff enemy ATTACK only, never health. Null Singularity was the one
card in the entire pool applying a persistent, un-cleansable enemy HEALTH
reduction (-1/-1, vs. Segmentation Fault's -1/-0) — and there is currently
no way to remove a Directive or cleanse a debuff anywhere in the card pool
(named in quality-debt.md's "Architectural gaps": "Directives can never
leave play"). Against a health-stacking archetype like Aegis specifically,
a persistent, unanswerable health drain is a structural hard counter, not
the "strong soft counter" design principle 3 asks for.

Change: Null Singularity's enemy aura, `attack: -1, health: -1` ->
`attack: -1, health: 0` (engine/src/data/nullCards.ts) — matching the
Segmentation Fault / Prime Aegis precedent. The ally buff and the card's
own 3/8 body are untouched.

=== Honest read: what two fix cycles did and didn't prove ===

- Aegis vs Null remains double-flagged after BOTH owner-directed fix steps:
  74.7-79.0% Null-favoured, versus the 75.0-81.3% this session started from.
  That is a small, real, measured improvement (mostly in the RandomAgent
  number, 81.3% -> 79.0%), not a meaningful fix. Per this project's own
  precedent (2026-09-15 owner feedback approving session 7's "stop after
  one measure-then-fix cycle rather than chasing an exact target"), and
  having now done the exact two-step process the owner specified for this
  pairing rather than guessing indefinitely, this session stops here on
  Aegis-vs-Null rather than continuing to nerf Null card by card.
- Both changes are real, defensible design fixes in their own right,
  independent of whether they moved this one number: Kernel Panic no longer
  unconditionally hard-counters any unit regardless of size (verified by a
  real unit test, not just the aggregate win rate), and Null Singularity no
  longer holds a uniquely uncounterable persistent health-drain that no
  other card in the pool has. Neither should be reverted just because the
  Aegis-vs-Null number didn't move much — they fixed the specific mechanics
  the owner named as problems.
- The more useful finding from this session's instrumentation: Aegis vs
  Null does NOT reduce to one or two bad Null numbers, and nerfing Null
  further card-by-card has diminishing, sometimes off-target returns (Step
  2's Null Singularity change moved Cortex-vs-Null more than it moved
  Aegis-vs-Null, the pairing it was aimed at). This matches
  balance-report-002's own "honest read" hypothesis, now with real evidence
  behind it rather than a guess: Aegis's whole plan (stack value into a
  small number of evolved/tooled units, answer little else) may be
  structurally exposed to *any* deck with steady removal + attrition, not
  specifically over-tuned Null numbers. The next real step for this
  specific matchup is most likely giving Aegis's own kit more independent
  answers (interaction, not just more health to stack), not further Null
  nerfs — but that's a card-set-design change, not "adjust Null's numbers,"
  so it's named here as the honest next step rather than attempted
  unilaterally this session.
- Swarm-vs-Aegis (61.3% Swarm, named as debt since session 7) and the
  single-flagged Cortex pairings (Cortex-vs-Aegis 70.0% Aggressive,
  Cortex-vs-Swarm 72.0% Random) are all still untouched — correctly out of
  scope for this session, which was scoped to exactly the owner's
  Aegis-vs-Null direction, not a full re-run of roadmap item 3.
