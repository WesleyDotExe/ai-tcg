## Reading it (session 81)

- The rule-change rebuild burst (quality-debt, session 80) queues; it doesn't stall. Every agent deck asking at
  once costs about 3.6 s of wall time per deck on 3 worker threads: 31 s for 8 decks, 91 s for 24, 172 s for 48.
  It grows linearly; the first round after a rule change waits that long before pairing. Throughout, the owner
  API kept answering: p99 6-10 ms, worst 67 ms, with no connection resets.
- Playing a round's matches happens on the main thread, before they are shown: 0.1 s for 4 matches, 0.4 s for
  24 (about 17 ms per match, house agents). That is the one synchronous block the API sees.
- What the stall column can't show: the 10 ms timer reports a stall when it next fires. A stall shorter than the
  phase it falls in is counted there, but a phase that is one synchronous block (the matches) ends before its
  timer fires, so its stall lands in the next phase or nowhere. Its upper bound is the phase's wall time: at most
  0.4 s at 48 owners.
- Not measured: a production-sized population (hundreds of owners), player owners' 2x votes, network agents
  (seated over WebSocket, not in-thread). At about 17 ms per match, the in-thread block would reach 1 s near 120
  matches per round. That is a number to re-check before the public launch, not a Season 0 problem.
- Machine: 4 CPUs (3 build workers). CI and production differ; re-run with
  `npm run measure:ladder-stall -w server -- <owners ...>`.
