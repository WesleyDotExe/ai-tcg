HONEST READ
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. **The ring is not within band on any of the six pairings yet, but the
   two authorized changes produced real, multi-policy movement in the
   places they targeted, not noise.** Aegis-vs-Null moved down under all
   three policies (a first — prior single-card nerfs, per session 16's own
   report, mostly moved only the Random number). Swarm-vs-Null moved toward
   parity under all three policies, and stopped being a triple-flag. Both
   changes are working as designed, just not far enough yet to land inside
   the 55-60%/45-55% bands.

2. **Per this entry's own instruction ("if Null vs Aegis lands in 55-60%,
   stop"), it did not land in band, so nothing further was attempted
   unilaterally** — no further Null nerfs, no Cortex nerfs (explicitly
   forbidden by this entry), no additional Swarm cards beyond the two
   proposed. This session did exactly the two authorized changes, measured
   honestly, and stops here, consistent with this project's established
   "measure, report, don't chase" pattern (see e.g. the 2026-09-15 owner
   feedback approving exactly this discipline).

3. **A real, unintended side effect: Cortex-vs-Null moved from 55.7% to
   60.7% under Aggressive** — a brand new single-policy flag that didn't
   exist before this session (Random and Lookahead are unaffected/moved the
   other way, so the cross-check doesn't trip). Plausible mechanism: raising
   Null's two most powerful cards' costs makes Null's early-to-mid game
   slightly weaker specifically against Cortex's own efficient curve, under
   the policy that plays greediest-affordable-card. Not chased or reverted
   — it's a single-policy signal, not a cross-check flag, and undoing a
   just-authorized change over one policy's number would be exactly the
   card-by-card chasing this project's own feedback history keeps warning
   against. Named honestly rather than silently left.

4. **Kernel Panic and Null Singularity are still extreme win-rate outliers
   WHEN PLAYED, even after the cost increase** (Kernel Panic: up to 86.4%
   played-win-rate under Random, 74.0% Aggressive, 69.4% Lookahead; Null
   Singularity: up to 78.7%/74.7%/67.3%). Raising their cost reduced HOW
   OFTEN they get played (both cards' "played" sample sizes dropped
   noticeably versus balance-report-010's numbers — Kernel Panic
   337->216 games played under Random, for instance) — which is exactly why
   Aegis-vs-Null's aggregate win rate moved down — but did not reduce their
   power once they land. This is expected and named, not a surprise: a cost
   increase is a frequency lever, not a power-level fix. If a future session
   wants Aegis-vs-Null fully in band, this per-card table suggests these two
   cards' raw stats/effects, not their cost, are the next lever — but that
   is new work, not something this entry authorized, so it is flagged here
   rather than attempted.

5. **Two of the "across" (should-be-even) pairings drifted further from
   band this session, in different directions, on different policies** —
   Cortex-vs-Aegis is unaffected (both untouched by this session's cards)
   but Swarm-vs-Aegis moved further Swarm-favoured under Aggressive (61.7%
   -> 64.7%) since the new Swarm cards are real, generically-valued board
   presence that helps Swarm everywhere, not just against Null. This is a
   known, accepted tradeoff of a systemic (not matchup-specific) fix — the
   entry asked for cards that make Swarm's kit better at going wide, not a
   Null-only answer, and a card that's good against Null's removal is also
   just a somewhat better card in general. Named rather than hidden.

6. **Cortex-vs-Swarm, the other triple-flagged pairing, was NOT specifically
   targeted by either authorized change and correctly didn't move toward
   band** — if anything it moved slightly further from band under Aggressive
   (65.3% -> 70.0%) and Random (66.7% -> 69.0%), while improving under
   Lookahead (65.3% -> 60.3%, still just over the flag line). The entry is
   explicit that Cortex is not to be nerfed and that Cortex-vs-Aegis
   softening (not Cortex-vs-Swarm) is the on-target consequence to expect
   from fixing Swarm. This pairing remains genuinely open, unassigned to
   either faction, per the entry's own framing ("only revisit Cortex if it
   still beats Aegis once Swarm is fixed" — Swarm is not yet "fixed" by this
   measure, so that condition hasn't even been reached).
