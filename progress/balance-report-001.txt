AI-TCG — first cross-faction balance measurement (design principle 3)
Session 7, 2026-09-15. Generated by `npm run balance --workspace=engine`
(engine/src/dev/balanceReport.ts), 300 seeds per matchup.

DESIGN.md, design principle 3: "track whether one archetype dominates win
rates over time; if it does, that's a balance bug to fix, and the record
should show it being fixed." Three factions (Cortex, Swarm, Aegis) existed
since session 4 with zero balance measurement between them until this
session — this file is that measurement, plus one real fix, cycle shown.

Method: each matchup runs the SAME agent policy on both sides (either
AggressiveAgent-vs-AggressiveAgent or RandomAgent-vs-RandomAgent), so the
only things that differ between games are the two factions' card pools and
seed-driven shuffle/draw/random-target order — agent skill is not a
confound. Which faction plays first alternates by seed to cancel a real,
separate first-player-advantage effect (measured directly via the mirror
matchups below). A pairing is "flagged" at >=60% win rate for either side;
a pairing flagged under BOTH agent policies is treated as strong evidence
of a genuinely dominant archetype (an agent-heuristic quirk favoring one
faction's play pattern would usually only show up under one policy, not
both).

=== BEFORE (initial measurement, card data as shipped through session 6) ===

AggressiveAgent vs AggressiveAgent:
  Cortex 191-109 Swarm  (63.7% / 36.3%)  <-- FLAG
  Cortex 214-86  Aegis  (71.3% / 28.7%)  <-- FLAG
  Swarm  184-116 Aegis  (61.3% / 38.7%)  <-- FLAG
  mirrors: Cortex 49.7% / Swarm 48.3% / Aegis 48.0% first-player win rate
  (confirms no first-player-advantage confound — all ~50%)

RandomAgent vs RandomAgent:
  Cortex 222-77  Swarm  (74.0% / 25.7%, 1 draw)  <-- FLAG
  Cortex 181-118 Aegis  (60.3% / 39.3%, 1 draw)  <-- FLAG
  Swarm  138-162 Aegis  (46.0% / 54.0%)
  mirrors: Cortex 52.0% / Swarm 51.0% / Aegis 46.7% first-player win rate

Cross-check (flagged under BOTH policies): Cortex vs Swarm, AND Cortex vs
Aegis. Cortex beat both other factions, under both a greedy heuristic and
uniform-random play — strong, non-coincidental evidence of a dominant
archetype, exactly the failure mode design principle 3 names.

Root-cause reading of the Cortex card pool (engine/src/data/cards.ts):
  1. Overload Capacitor (CX-011, Rare, 1 copy): 5/5 stats for cost 3. The
     curve baseline elsewhere at cost 3 is ~6-7 combined attack+health
     (Firewall Sentinel 2/5 Guard, Broodcaller 3/3 + a draw condition,
     Watchtower Relay 2/4 + a recurring energy trigger) — 5/5 is a
     10-point stat total, well above that baseline. Its downside (1
     self-damage/turn for 3 turns, 3 total) is minor next to 5 health and
     doesn't meaningfully offset the raw stat lead.
  2. Interrupt Handler (CX-014) duplicates Diagnostic Pulse (CX-004)'s
     exact effect (2 damage to target enemy unit) at the same 1-mana cost,
     just at Instant speed instead of Sorcery — meaning the Cortex deck
     runs 6 copies (3+3) of "1 mana, 2 damage, precise target" between the
     two cards, plus 2 more copies of Cascade Purge (4-mana AOE, 2 damage
     to all enemies): 8/40 cards (20%) are cheap, precise or AOE removal.
     Neither Swarm nor Aegis has a comparable density of cheap *targeted*
     removal (Swarm's damage effects are all random-target or AOE-on-
     death/telegraph, never "pick your target" for 1 mana). Against
     Swarm's low-toughness board (many 1-3 health units) especially, this
     trivializes trades card-for-card at minimal cost.

=== FIX APPLIED ===

Two targeted, minimal changes to engine/src/data/cards.ts (no card removed,
no new mechanic — a stats/cost tuning pass, the kind design principle 3
explicitly calls for):
  1. Overload Capacitor: 5/5 -> 4/4 (same cost, same downside). Brings its
     stat total from 10 down to 8, still above the ~6-7 curve baseline
     (it's still a Rare bomb, deliberately) but no longer a clear outlier.
  2. Interrupt Handler: cost 1 -> 2. Keeps its identity (an Instant-speed
     reactive answer, mechanically distinct from Diagnostic Pulse's
     Sorcery-speed twin) while removing the "two separate 1-mana precise
     kill spells, 3 copies each" density that was likely the larger
     contributor given how much more often a deck sees 6 low-cost
     removal copies than 1 copy of a single Rare.

=== AFTER (re-measured with the fix in place) ===

AggressiveAgent vs AggressiveAgent:
  Cortex 174-126 Swarm  (58.0% / 42.0%)          -- below flag threshold
  Cortex 210-90  Aegis  (70.0% / 30.0%)  <-- FLAG
  Swarm  184-116 Aegis  (61.3% / 38.7%)  <-- FLAG (unchanged; Cortex wasn't involved)
  mirrors: Cortex 56.3% / Swarm 48.3% / Aegis 48.0% first-player win rate

RandomAgent vs RandomAgent:
  Cortex 216-83  Swarm  (72.0% / 27.7%, 1 draw)  <-- FLAG
  Cortex 164-136 Aegis  (54.7% / 45.3%)          -- below flag threshold
  Swarm  138-162 Aegis  (46.0% / 54.0%)
  mirrors: Cortex 52.3% / Swarm 51.0% / Aegis 46.7% first-player win rate

Cross-check (flagged under BOTH policies): NONE. Cortex vs Swarm dropped
out of the double-flag under Aggressive (63.7% -> 58.0%); Cortex vs Aegis
dropped out under Random (60.3% -> 54.7%). The strong signal (one faction
beating both others under both agent policies) is gone after this single
tuning pass.

=== Honest read: what this does and doesn't prove ===

- This is real, measured evidence a specific balance bug existed and that
  a specific, small change reduced it — not a claim that the three
  factions are now balanced. Cortex vs Aegis (Aggressive) and Cortex vs
  Swarm (Random) still individually clear the 60% flag threshold; they're
  just no longer double-flagged, which is the weaker (single-policy) signal
  this script's cross-check was built to distinguish from a genuine
  dominant archetype. Swarm vs Aegis (61.3% Swarm, both before and after,
  untouched by this session's changes since neither card involved is in
  either deck) is its own open flag, not investigated this session.
- Both dummy agents are unsophisticated (no lookahead, fixed heuristics),
  so these numbers describe how Cortex/Swarm/Aegis perform under greedy
  and random play specifically, not under skilled play — a real future
  agent might exploit or neutralize the current gaps differently.
- Sample size (300 seeds/matchup) is enough to separate a 60%+ signal from
  noise cleanly (as the before/after comparison shows) but this is a first
  measurement, not a standing regression gate — nothing currently re-runs
  this automatically the way matchSmoke.test.ts does for crash-safety.
- The mirror-match numbers (44-56% range) confirm first-player advantage
  is small and not confounding the cross-faction numbers, which is the
  main methodological risk this design was built to rule out.

Next step this leaves for a future session: decide whether to keep
iterating on Cortex-vs-Aegis and Cortex-vs-Swarm specifically (more
targeted card changes, same measure-then-fix cycle), or treat "no
double-flagged pairing" as good enough for the current, still-small card
pool and revisit once the sets are larger. Either is legitimate; this
session stopped after one real, evidence-backed fix cycle rather than
iterating to a specific target number, to keep the slice bounded.
