Reading: the whole move is Cortex's pilot (-3.3 with the old Aegis pilot, -3.0 with the new one; Aegis's
pilot changes nothing either way). The card whose use moved most is Mainframe Core (3-energy Directive,
"Your units get +1/+1"): played in 38.5% -> 45.7% of games. It is exactly the kind of card the old agent
under-valued (an aura counted for one turn only). Aegis played Static Ward Beacon (its own +0/+1 aura)
more too, but Aegis's side shows no net gain. So the old 52.2% measured Aegis against a Cortex that
under-played its best engine card; the soft counter was thinner than it looked all along.

Reading:
  B (+1/+0) is the only trial that restores Aegis > Cortex by a real amount in the gate (+3.7 [0.9, 6.5],
  to 53.0%, just above the 52.2% the owner accepted as OUT on 2026-10-04). It also moves Null ~ Cortex
  into band (48.3% IN) and leaves Cortex > Swarm inside its band (55.9%, CI straddling 55). It does not
  reach the 55-60% band; no single Mainframe Core change measured here does.
  A (cost 4) and C (one copy) move the edge less than noise.
  In signature mode B pushes Aegis > Cortex further out (71.5 -> 74.8, +3.4 [0.9, 5.8]); that edge is
  already far over its band there, and the CI signature check would fail on it, so shipping B needs
  both baselines re-recorded on purpose, as the gate's rules allow for an owner-approved change.
  Removing Mainframe Core entirely (context row) moves the gate edge about as much as B (+3.0), so
  B keeps the card and its role (wide board pressure) and takes out the +1 health. Why the health half
  matters against Aegis specifically is a reading, not measured here (a guess: Aegis's turrets and
  attackers deal 1-2 damage, which +1 health turns from kills into chip).
