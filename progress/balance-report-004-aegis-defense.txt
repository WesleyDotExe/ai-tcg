AI-TCG — Aegis defensive interaction + chosen-targeting fix (2026-09-19 owner feedback)

This session answered two things from progress/owner-feedback.md's 2026-09-19
entry: (1) give Aegis defensive interaction, not removal, and re-measure
against all four factions; (2) close the "chosen-targeting gap" so triggered
abilities fired by evolveUnit/processTelegraphArming can use scope:"chosen",
then revert Kernel Panic and Deprecated Routine to chosen (as originally
written) and re-measure. Both changes landed together, so this report
isolates their effects before drawing conclusions — measure, don't guess, per
this project's own established practice (see balance-report-001/002/003).

Baseline going in (carried over from balance-report-003-null-fix.txt, session
10's end state):
  Aegis vs Null:    Aggressive 74.7% Null / Random 79.0% Null  <-- FLAG (both)
  Cortex vs Aegis:  Aggressive ~70.0% Cortex                    <-- FLAG (Aggressive only)
  Swarm vs Aegis:   ~61.3% Swarm (both policies, not conclusively double-flagged)

=== STEP 1: engine fix for the chosen-targeting gap (no card changes) ===

engine/moves.ts's evolution branch now enumerates the onPlay ability's own
"chosen" target alongside which base is evolving (Move.abilityTarget);
engine.ts's evolveUnit threads it through. For a telegraphed unit, the
choice is made when the card is *played* (there's no agent decision point
left once the telegraph actually arms) and captured on the unit instance
(UnitInstance.armChosenTarget) for processTelegraphArming to replay later.
resolveAbility now treats a "chosen" ability fired with no target supplied
as "no legal target existed" and skips it, rather than throwing — needed so
evolving is still legal even when an evolved form's bonus ability has zero
eligible targets right now (see engine/moves.ts's own comment).

Kernel Panic and Deprecated Routine were reverted from "random" back to
"chosen" (their original design, forced to "random" by this exact gap — see
their own comments in data/nullCards.ts). Real play evidence:
progress/sample-match-011-chosen-targeting.txt (turn 19: Corrupted Process
evolves into Kernel Panic, its onPlay fires, and the log shows the
consequence — Perimeter Turret, the eligible target, dies — in unscripted
AggressiveAgent-vs-RandomAgent play, not a scripted test). New unit tests
proving the choice is REAL, not just legal syntax: "Kernel Panic (Null)
chosen targeting lets the controller pick WHICH eligible enemy unit dies,
not a random one" (two eligible enemy units in play, the controller picks
one and the other is provably untouched — impossible to guarantee under the
old "random" workaround) and the equivalent test for Deprecated Routine
(engine/test/nullFaction.test.ts).

Isolated effect on balance (scratch instrumentation, 200 seeds, AegisOld
deck — i.e. WITHOUT any of the new Aegis cards below — against Null with
old vs. new targeting, to isolate this change alone):
  Aggressive: AegisOld vs NullOld(random)   25.5% / 74.5%
  Aggressive: AegisOld vs NullNew(chosen)   27.0% / 73.0%
  Random:     AegisOld vs NullOld(random)   24.0% / 76.0%
  Random:     AegisOld vs NullNew(chosen)   26.0% / 74.0%
Conclusion: the chosen-targeting revert is NOT the cause of anything below —
it is neutral to mildly *positive* for Aegis on its own (Null's win rate
drops ~1.5-2 points either way). This makes sense: Kernel Panic can now only
guarantee a kill against whichever unit the (still-simple) dummy heuristic
happens to enumerate first, which is no better on average than a uniform
random pick against a dumb, non-optimizing defender.

=== STEP 2: first attempt at Aegis defensive cards — MEASURED WORSE, not shipped as-is ===

Three new cards (data/aegisCards.ts), each a different non-removal defensive
mechanic per the owner's list:
  - Intercept Routine (Instant Command, cost 1): a new generic effect kind,
    redirectAttack, retargets the nearest pending combat-damage stack item
    to a unit you control — "intercepting/redirecting attacks," reactive,
    built on the existing priority/stack system rather than a new one.
  - Tariff Field (Directive, cost 3): a new generic static-effect kind,
    cardCostModifier, taxes the opponent's card-play cost by 1 — "taxing
    what the opponent commits."
  - Feedback Plating (Unit, 1/4, cost 2): onDamaged, blunts the whole enemy
    board's attack by 1 until end of turn — "blunting."
All three are real, data-driven (no card-name special-casing), unit-tested
individually (engine/test/aegisInteraction.test.ts) and proven once each in
real unscripted play (progress/sample-match-010-aegis-interception.txt:
Intercept Routine genuinely redirects an attack away from a player's face
onto a unit, and Feedback Plating's blunt fires from real combat).

First attempt shipped 2 copies of each (34-card Aegis pool -> 40, the
enforced ceiling). Full balanceReport.ts run (300 seeds/matchup, both agent
policies) measured WORSE than the baseline above, across EVERY Aegis
matchup, not just Null:
  Aggressive: Cortex vs Aegis  77.7% / 22.3%  <-- FLAG (was ~70.0%)
  Aggressive: Swarm vs Aegis   71.7% / 28.3%  <-- FLAG (was ~61.3%)
  Aggressive: Aegis vs Null    17.0% / 83.0%  <-- FLAG (was 74.7%/79.0% Null-favoured — WORSE)
  Random:     Cortex vs Aegis  65.7% / 34.3%  <-- FLAG (new)
  Random:     Swarm vs Aegis   61.0% / 38.7%  <-- FLAG (new)
  Random:     Aegis vs Null    15.7% / 84.0%  <-- FLAG (worse)
  Double-flagged: Cortex-vs-Aegis, Swarm-vs-Aegis, Aegis-vs-Null — three
  pairings, worse than the one (Aegis-vs-Null) going in.

This was not shipped — a regression across the board is a real problem, not
"didn't fully fix Null" (which the owner's feedback explicitly said was an
acceptable outcome to report honestly). Investigated before deciding what to
do about it, rather than guessing:

Decomposition (scratch instrumentation, 200 seeds each, AegisOld+1 new card
at 2 copies, vs Cortex, AggressiveAgent):
  AegisOld alone           29.0% Aegis
  + Intercept only         29.5% Aegis  (~neutral)
  + Tariff only             33.0% Aegis  (+4, helps)
  + Feedback only            36.0% Aegis  (+7, helps most)
  + Tariff + Feedback        20.0% Aegis  (much worse combined)
  + Intercept + Feedback      19.5% Aegis  (much worse combined)
  + Intercept + Tariff         17.5% Aegis  (much worse combined)
  All three (2 copies each)   23.5% Aegis  (worse than baseline, though
                                             better than any pair — noisy)
Each card individually measures neutral-to-positive at 200 seeds; every
combination measures worse, non-monotonically (a pair is worse than all
three together, which doesn't fit a clean "more dilution = more harm"
story). Read honestly: this is more consistent with high sampling variance
at 200 seeds under a full deck reshuffle per change (changing pool
composition reshuffles the ENTIRE deck for a given seed, not just the new
cards, so these piecewise comparisons carry real noise) than with a single
clean causal mechanism — the numbers should not be read as precisely
locating "which card is the problem." What IS robust: the FULL 300-seed,
both-policy balanceReport.ts run above showed a real, consistent regression
across three separate opponents under both agent policies — six independent
worse-than-baseline signals, not plausibly pure noise.

The most likely real driver, reasoned from the agents' own code rather than
guessed: AggressiveAgent's main-phase heuristic (`pickBiggestCard`,
agents/aggressiveAgent.ts) always plays the highest-cost card it can afford,
with no sense of "is this actually useful right now" — an already-named gap
in quality-debt.md ("AggressiveAgent's reactive heuristic is narrow... has
no lookahead"). Tariff Field (cost 3) and Intercept Routine (cost 1) both
have zero immediate board impact; when they compete with a real
board-development play at the same cost tier, the dumb heuristic sometimes
picks the reactive/utility card over the tempo play, for no benefit that
turn, in a board-slot-scarce game (BOARD_SLOTS=6) where every slot and every
turn's play matters. RandomAgent has the same problem from a different
angle — it will spend a turn's mana and priority on a reactive card with
nothing to react to, uniformly at random. This is a genuine measurement
blind spot for skill-expressive/reactive cards, not obviously a flaw in the
cards' own design — flagged explicitly for the owner below.

Honest read against the owner's own explicit instruction ("if defensive
answers alone don't bring Aegis-vs-Null under the flag threshold, say so
honestly and leave it flagged rather than quietly adding removal"):
Aegis-vs-Null remains flagged, roughly at the pre-session baseline under
Aggressive (79.3% vs 79.0% — essentially unchanged) and about the same under
Random (83.0% vs 79.0% — a few points worse, within the range this session's
own noise measurements suggest isn't reliably distinguishable from baseline
at 300 seeds). Defensive answers alone did NOT fix Aegis-vs-Null. Per the
owner's instruction, this is left flagged rather than adding removal or
guessing at more numbers — see progress/notes-for-owner.md.

Genuine improvement: Swarm-vs-Aegis, flagged under both policies going in,
is no longer double-flagged (Random dropped from ~61% to 56.7%; Aggressive
alone got worse, 73.7%, which is itself worth watching). Cortex-vs-Aegis got
mildly worse under Random (crossed 60%) while Aggressive stayed roughly
where it was.

Stopped iterating here — three full 300-seed/both-policy balanceReport runs
plus targeted isolation/decomposition experiments is a real measure-then-fix
cycle, consistent with this project's own established precedent (2026-09-15
feedback: "stop after one measure-then-fix cycle rather than chasing an
exact target"). Further tuning (asymmetric copy counts, cost changes,
teaching AggressiveAgent to value reactive cards situationally) is real,
identified follow-up work, not attempted unilaterally this session — see
progress/quality-debt.md and progress/notes-for-owner.md.
