The noise finding (new, and it changes how the ring should be read):
  300 seeds is about a 2.9-point standard error. Over seeds 1-900 the BASELINE Null edges read:
  Null vs Cortex 42.1%, Null vs Swarm 36.6%, Null vs Aegis 37.3% (lookaheadEdgeCheck Null 1 900;
  the Swarm and Aegis numbers are unchanged by this slice). Null is below 45% against every
  faction, including Aegis, the edge the ring says Null should WIN at 55-60% (report 015 showed
  45.0% on 300 seeds). Null is the weakest faction by a wider margin than the 300-seed reports
  suggested, and that one fact is behind all three of its out-of-band edges.

Why Null-vs-Cortex could not be held while fixing Swarm (measured, not assumed):
  Ablation, Lookahead, seeds 1-300, one card removed at a time from cut 2 (points of Cortex win
  rate lost vs Swarm / Aegis / Null): Rate Limiter -9.0 / +2.4 / +1.3 (the one truly
  Swarm-selective card); Load Balancer -10.7 / -3.0 / -3.7; Quorum Arbiter -6.7 / -2.6 / -5.7;
  Hotfix and Deadlock Detector within noise everywhere. Separately, replacing ANY of the weak
  trimmed cards (Backup Protocol, Cortex Engineer) with anything moved vs-Null by +5-10.
  Across 150 random copy mixes (a search on seeds 1-300, checked on seeds 301-900), Cortex's
  rate vs Null tracked its rate vs Swarm almost 1:1, about 5-10 points higher. No mix put
  Swarm >= 55 and Null <= 55 at once, and the search's best-looking mixes regressed on the
  held-out seeds (search overfit). Board measurements explain why card design can't separate
  them much: Swarm fields 1-health units 34% of the time vs Null 18% (Aegis 7%), and 4+ units
  30% vs Null 21% (Aegis 18%). The difference is real but small.
  So Cortex-vs-Null follows Null's overall weakness. The owner's sanctioned lever is already
  queued: Null's next slice trims its filler back toward ~36 cards (consistency, not stats).

Journey (four measured cuts; cut 2 shipped):
  Cut 1: RL threshold 4 x1, LB x2, Hotfix x2, Deadlock x2, Quorum x1; trims included Watchtower
         Relay. Lookahead 300: Swarm 56.0, Aegis 39.3 (Aegis 60.7, just out), Null 60.3.

         Lookahead 300: Swarm 58.7, Aegis 43.3, Null 63.7; 900: 54.1 / 41.6 / 61.1.
  Card-design variants tried on top of cut 2 and dropped (all within noise on every edge):
         RL threshold 4; Load Balancer hitting all enemy units with 1 max health; Load Balancer
         restricted to <=2 max health; Quorum Arbiter at 4 or 5 health, base 0 or 2, cost 4.
  Copy-mix search: 150 mixes evaluated, none with Swarm >= 55 and Null <= 55 together.
         Held-out check (seeds 301-900) of cut 2 and two of the best-looking search mixes:
         best search mix 59.0/40.3/58.7 -> 52.5/39.0/63.0; another 55.0/36.3/55.7 ->
         48.0/37.2/58.0; cut 2 58.7/43.3/63.7 -> 51.8/40.7/59.8. Everything regressed toward
         the mean. Cut 2 held out best (Aegis in band, lowest vs-Null of the mixes that kept
         Swarm above 50) and was not picked by the search, so it is not a seed-fit. Shipped.

Per-card notes (Lookahead, played): Quorum Arbiter 58.8%, Load Balancer 57.0%, Deadlock Detector
50.0%, Hotfix 49.7%, Rate Limiter 41.7%. Rate Limiter's low overall rate is the cost of being
selective: it idles against narrow Aegis/Null boards, and ablation shows it is the card doing the
Swarm-specific work. Hotfix is cast on Cortex's own turn 260 times for every 2 on the opponent's
(300 games per matchup): the LookaheadAgent values a temporary buff the moment it has a target,
not when combat is pending. Swarm's Reflex Spike shows the same pattern (150 own-turn casts, 0
reactive). That is an instrument blind spot, left for a deliberate agent pass because fixing it
moves Swarm's numbers too.
